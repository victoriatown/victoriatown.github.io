Graphic Novel: Motherhood in Crisis Response

The interactive graphic novel “Graphic Novel: Motherhood in Crisis” by Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager, and Saidu Bah uses visual communication and interactive design to create a deeply emotional storytelling experience. Rather than presenting information in a traditional article format, the website combines comic-style graphics, speech bubbles, scrolling interactions, and sound to immerse the audience in the lives of women in Sierra Leone facing dangerous pregnancies and limited healthcare access.

One of the most effective design choices is the use of illustrated graphics instead of photography. The hand-drawn comic style humanises the women and makes the stories feel personal and intimate. The illustrations also help communicate difficult topics, such as childbirth complications, poverty, and grief, in a way that feels emotional without being overly graphic or confronting. The muted colour palette of earthy browns, reds, and dark tones, reflects the seriousness of the issue and creates a dull mood throughout the experience.

The speech bubbles are also an important technique as instead of large blocks of text, the dialogue appears naturally beside the characters, making the reader feel as though they are listening directly to the women tell their stories. This conversational style creates a stronger emotional connection and makes the information easier to absorb. The speech bubbles also help control pacing because the reader moves through the narrative one moment at a time, whilst also seeing visuals that break up some text, is important as it prompts viewers to reflect rather than rushing through the information.

The interactive layout of the website also plays a major role in storytelling. By clicking through different sections, I believe this creates a sense of progression through each woman’s journey. Unlike a static article, the scrolling experience creates more of a connection as illustrations move across the screen and transitions to reveal new scenes. This interactivity keeps the audience engaged and makes them active participants in the story rather than passive readers. The website design carefully balances text and imagery so the screen never feels overcrowded, allowing the audience to focus on key emotional moments.

Overall, I believe “Motherhood in Crisis” demonstrates how news stories can combine graphic design, storytelling, and user experience to communicate serious global issues in a powerful way. By using illustrations, speech bubbles, scrolling interactions, and multimedia elements, this can really transform the factual reporting into an emotional and immersive experience that leaves a lasting impact on the audience.
